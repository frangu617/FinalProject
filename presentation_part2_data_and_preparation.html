A little professor tip

Speaker notes · About 30 seconds

Our data comes from the UCI Poker Hand dataset. Every row describes five cards, giving us a suit and rank for each card, plus the correct hand category. That means ten inputs and one answer. The example here contains a pair of tens, so the answer is one pair. The supplied files contain just over one million labeled hands.

Speaker notes · About 30 seconds

Before modeling, we checked the training records for missing values, valid codes, repeated cards within a hand, and conflicting labels. Those checks passed. We treated the same five cards in a different order as the same hand. Removing one hundred and twelve extra copies left almost twenty-five thousand distinct hands. This keeps duplicate hands from appearing in both training and validation.

Speaker notes · About 30 seconds

We used eighty percent for training and twenty percent for validation, preserving category proportions. The supplied test file is reserved for final evaluation, although earlier descriptive summaries included it. Suit and rank categories are encoded within the training pipeline. These steps improve our comparison, but rare hands remain a limitation: we have only four distinct royal flushes. Next, we will look at the imbalance between common and rare hands.

Sources and preparation notes

Dataset definitions: UCI Machine Learning Repository — Poker Hand. Cattral, R. & Oppacher, F. (2002). DOI: 10.24432/C5KW38.

Preparation counts and checks were verified using the training notebook. The reproducible split uses a random seed of 42. Valid code ranges are suits 1–4, ranks 1–13, and categories 0–9. Duplicate checks compare the same five cards regardless of order; they do not group different hands related by renaming suits. The retained card order is still used by the models.

Both supplied files were included in earlier descriptive exploration. Test results stay out of model selection; the notebook’s full test evaluation is disabled by default and checks overlap with development hands when enabled. Label consistency was checked for identical training hands; a complete audit against poker rules remains a separate step.
